Leading paths: a working view for discussion

Our research points to combinations of providers rather than a single one. Three structures stand out:

Path A

An impact manager runs a note vehicle. It accepts loans and grants and lends to funds abroad.

Candidates: Total Impact Capital, Calvert Impact (via its RIA), Candide Group, Social Finance.

Precedents: Olamina Fund, Total Impact Notes.

Path B

AEF's own capital sits in a DAF that invests in a Path A vehicle. This gets around the fact that a DAF can't accept loans.

Candidates: ImpactAssets, Impact Charitable, Realize Impact.

Precedent: ImpactAssets' investment in Olamina Fund.

Path C

A DAF-hosted program, with underwriting by AEF or an outside advisor. Simpler and lighter-touch, but suited only to grant capital.

Precedents: Abundance Circle, WRI TerraFund.

Current working view

  • Lead pairing (Path B): Total Impact Capital as manager, with ImpactAssets holding AEF's own capital. Total Impact Capital is the only candidate already lending to intermediary funds abroad at this scale; ImpactAssets has played this investor and administrator role before.
  • Main alternatives: Social Finance, if inflows mix grants, recoverable grants and loans (international scope still to confirm); and Calvert Impact, which has the strongest credit capacity if the pilot is designed to grow beyond $5M.

Statuses reflect what public sources confirm, not a ranking. Comments welcome.

Pilot parameters per AEF / to confirm

  • Size: around $5M.
  • Inflows: may combine grants, recoverable grants and loans.
  • Outflows: almost all international, all debt (possibly subordinated). Some borrowers may have US entities.
  • AEF participates in decisions, but underwriting is done by a third party: either the host or a separate advisor.
  • End state: a permanent home of roughly the same size, or a vehicle that grows and/or spins out.
  • Only options that return capital are considered. A DAF counts as a partial fit: AEF doesn't get the money back, but it stays in an AEF-advised DAF to recycle or grant later.

Screening criteria

  1. Returns or recycles capital (no pure grant pass-through)
  2. Can lend internationally
  3. Can accept mixed inflows: grants, recoverable grants, loans
  4. Underwrites, or partners with an underwriter
  5. Workable at ~$5M
Structural point: a DAF can only accept gifts. It can't take loans, and it can't repay a donor's recoverable grant. So a DAF host works for AEF's own capital, but outside lenders would need a second entity.
